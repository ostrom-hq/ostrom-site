A receipt is a small JSON document, signed by an Ostrom instance, that says what happened during a piece of agent work.

A receipt records:

- what it is about: a session, a gate, a task or a workflow run;
- who started the work, and with which agent and model;
- which rules applied, each with the fingerprint of its exact text;
- every yes or no given, by whom and when;
- what it cost, when it is known.

## What a receipt proves

A valid signature proves two things: the receipt was signed by the holder of the instance's private key, and not one byte of it changed since. Change a name, a date or a cost, and the check fails.

It does not prove that what the receipt says is true beyond that. A receipt is as trustworthy as the instance that signed it and the safety of its key. See [security](/docs/security).

## Check a receipt

You need the receipt and the public key of the instance that signed it. The check runs offline: nothing is sent anywhere.

- By hand, with a short script: [receipt format v1](/docs/receipts/format#verify-a-receipt-yourself) gives a complete verifier and the rules it follows.
- With `ostrom verify`: Coming soon, with the CLI.

## Get the instance's public key

Every instance publishes the keys it signs with, without sign-in, at `GET /api/v1/instance/keys`. Replace `ostrom.example.com` with your instance:

```sh notest="needs a running Ostrom server"
curl -s https://ostrom.example.com/api/v1/instance/keys
```

The answer lists every key the instance has used, oldest first. Each key has a `keyId`, the `kid` a receipt names; an `algorithm`, `ed25519`; the public key itself in `publicKeyPem`, in PEM (SPKI) form, the form the verifier takes; and `firstSeenAt`, when the instance first used it. Keep the key of the `kid` your receipt names. `GET /api/v1/instance` gives the key the instance signs with now, under `signingKey`.

Fetch the key once, from a source you trust, and keep it: a key fetched again at the moment you check a receipt only proves what the server says at that moment. See [security](/docs/security).
