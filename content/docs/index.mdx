Ostrom runs a company's AI coding agents in the cloud and makes them follow the team's rules. Every change they make leaves a receipt, and the few that matter wait for a person's yes.

Ostrom is open source. These pages describe only what is in the released code. A page marked Coming soon describes a part that is not released yet; a page marked Partial says which of its parts are still Coming soon. Both fill in when the code ships.

## Start here

The [quickstart](/docs/quickstart) takes you from nothing to your first yes. It is not released yet; until it is, the
part you can use is the [receipt format](/docs/receipts/format): read a receipt and check it yourself, without Ostrom.

## The words Ostrom uses

**Agent.** A coding agent such as Claude Code or Codex, started by a person on their machine or run in the cloud.

**Harness.** Everything an agent starts with: your team's rules, the checks that run on its actions, and its settings. Ostrom builds it from your workflows, so every agent of the team starts with the same one.

**Workflow.** How your team ships, written down: steps, checks, gates and notifications, in the order they happen.

**Gate.** A point in a workflow where an action waits for a person. Pushing to `main` is a typical gate. Most actions have no gate: a yes asked for everything becomes a reflex.

**Yes.** A named person's approval of one gate, given with the evidence in front of them. It is recorded in a receipt.

**Receipt.** A signed record of what happened: who started the work, which agent and rules were involved, and who said yes. Anyone with the instance's public key can check it, offline. See [receipts](/docs/receipts).

**Organization.** Your company in Ostrom: its people, its repositories, its workflows and its receipts. Each organization's data is kept apart from every other organization's.
